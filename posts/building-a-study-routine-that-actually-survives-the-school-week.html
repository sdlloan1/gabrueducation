Building a study routine that actually survives the school week

Most timetables collapse by Wednesday. Here is the simpler routine we give every new student at Gabru Education — and why it holds.

By Neha Bansal

Every student we meet has already made a timetable. Most of them have abandoned it by Wednesday evening. The problem is rarely laziness — it is that the timetable was built for an ideal week, not for a real one.

Start with the week you actually have

Before writing a single study slot, list the fixed parts of your week: school, coaching, travel, meals, and the two or three evenings that are already spoken for. What remains is your real study time. Be honest about it — a plan built on eight imaginary hours a day is worse than no plan at all.

  • Two focused hours beat five distracted ones
  • Keep one evening completely free every week
  • Never schedule a subject for the last slot of the day if it needs your full attention

Give every subject a fixed home

Instead of deciding each evening what to study, decide once. Physics after dinner, Maths before school, revision on Sunday morning. When the decision is already made, starting becomes much easier — and that is usually the whole battle.

The aim is not a perfect week. It is a week you can repeat.

Review on Sunday, change one thing

At the end of each week, ask a single question: which slot did I actually miss? Fix that one slot. Do not redesign the entire plan — that is how students end up rebuilding a timetable every Sunday instead of following one.

Make the doubt list visible

Keep a single page where you write down every question you could not answer. Bring it to class. A doubt that stays in your head for three days turns into a chapter you avoid for three months.

Why this works

A routine is not a punishment. It is a promise you make to yourself about when you will show up. Make it small enough that the promise is easy to keep, and the marks follow on their own.
